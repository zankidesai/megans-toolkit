Bilateral Tapping

Alternating left-right stimulation calms the amygdala — same principle as EMDR therapy. Follow the light and tap along.

How to tap

Rest your hands on your thighs or knees. When the left hand lights up — tap your left knee. When right lights up — tap your right knee. Keep it gentle and rhythmic. You can also cross your arms and tap your shoulders.

Why this works

Bilateral (left-right) stimulation activates both brain hemispheres simultaneously, reducing the intensity of distressing emotions. It's the same mechanism behind EMDR therapy, developed for trauma and hyperarousal — and you don't need a therapist to use it.

Outdoors version

Walking does the same thing — your left and right foot strike is bilateral. Walk at a medium pace, no music, just feel the alternating movement. Tapping a trail marker, a rock, or your hiking poles alternately also counts.

OCD — Intrusive Thoughts

Built specifically for your brain. Understanding what's happening is part of defusing it.

🧠 What's actually happening

All brains generate intrusive thoughts. In OCD, your brain mislabels them as significant or dangerous and fires an alarm. The more you push the thought away or neutralize it, the more your brain treats it as a real threat — and loops it back louder.

The thought is not a desire. Not a prediction. Not who you are. It is misfiring threat detection. That is all.

When a thought intrudes

The DARE Response

D
Defuse — label it without engaging

Say mentally: "There's a thought." Not "I'm thinking that I might ___." Just: "There's a thought." You are the observer. The thought is not you.

A
Allow — don't fight it

Fighting gives it power. Let it exist without doing anything about it. "Okay, you're here. I'm not going to argue with you."

R
Redirect — move attention, not thoughts

Name 3 things you see. Press your feet to the floor. Take one slow breath. The thought can stay in the background — you're choosing where to aim your focus.

E
Engage — return to your life

The thought may still be at the edges. You don't need it gone to move forward. The more you practice returning without engaging, the faster the loop dies.

Why NOT to reassure yourself

Each time you seek reassurance, you teach your brain that the thought required a response — OCD gets stronger. The goal is to tolerate uncertainty without treating it as an emergency.

Urge surfing

Ride the wave — don't act on it

Compulsive urges peak and fall within 20–40 minutes whether you act on them or not. Go to the Timer tab and set a delay. Every surf weakens that neural pathway a little more.

The science

This is Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) — the gold-standard OCD treatment. You don't need a therapist. Tolerating discomfort without the compulsion is itself the therapy.

Body Shaking (TRE)
Discharge energy · 60–90 sec
▶
  • 1

    Stand feet hip-width, knees slightly bent.

  • 2

    Start gently bouncing your legs — heels barely rising.

  • 3

    Let shaking travel up through hips, torso, arms. Don't control it.

  • 4

    Shake for 60–90 seconds. Your legs might shake harder on their own — that's right.

  • 5

Why (TRE)

Animals shake after a threat to discharge adrenaline. Humans learned to suppress this. TRE recreates that discharge and completes the stress cycle that rage leaves unfinished.

Nutrition for your brain

What to eat and why

No rules — just what your brain runs best on. More of these = more stable mood, better energy, less reactivity.

🥩
Protein at every meal

Proteins are amino acid precursors to serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine — the exact neurotransmitters your medications help support. Aim for 20–30g at breakfast especially: eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken, salmon, beans, lentils.

🐟
Omega-3 fatty acids

DHA and EPA directly support brain cell membrane function and reduce neuroinflammation. Studies show supplementation reduces bipolar episode frequency. Sources: salmon, sardines, walnuts, flaxseed, chia seeds. Or a fish oil supplement (1–2g/day).

🌱
Gut-brain connection

90% of your serotonin is made in your gut. Fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut) and fiber (fruits, veggies, legumes, oats) feed the gut bacteria that support serotonin production. Your gut literally affects your mood.

🩸
Blood sugar stability

Blood sugar crashes trigger cortisol spikes — which trigger anxiety and irritability. Don't skip meals. Pair carbs with protein or fat. Oatmeal alone spikes then crashes; oatmeal + peanut butter sustains. This one matters more than most people realize.

☕
Caffeine — with limits

Fine in the morning. Caffeine after 2pm disrupts sleep quality even if you fall asleep fine — and poor sleep is the fastest trigger for mood instability. Keep it to 1–2 cups before noon.

🍬
Ultra-processed food & sugar

High sugar intake increases systemic inflammation, which directly worsens depression and anxiety symptoms. Not "never eat it" — just notice how you feel 2 hours after. Your body gives clear feedback.

Why does this happen to me?

You deserve to understand what's going on in your own brain. None of this is a character flaw.

🧠 What bipolar disorder actually does

Your amygdala fires more easily and takes longer to calm down. Your prefrontal cortex has a harder time putting the brakes on emotional reactions when you're activated. You are not overreacting — you are having a neurologically larger reaction to the same trigger. What you're building here is the skill of bridging the gap between the alarm firing and your response.

🔄 What hyper-arousal actually is

When activated, cortisol and adrenaline flood your system and blood literally moves away from your prefrontal cortex and toward your muscles. This is why "just calm down" doesn't work — you're physiologically prepared to fight or flee, not think. Every tool here targets your physiology directly, working with your body not against it.

🌀 OCD intrusive thoughts — the loop

Each time you engage with the thought — reassuring, checking, analyzing — your brain logs: that thought required a response. The loop gets stronger. The path through OCD is changing your relationship to the thought — letting it exist without treating it as an emergency.

💊 What your medications are doing

Your mood stabilizer reduces the abnormal electrical activity in the brain that drives mood episodes — especially the lows and rapid cycling. It raises the floor without sedating you.

Your SSRI increases serotonin availability over time, supporting emotional regulation, impulse control, and OCD symptom management. The two work on different systems and complement each other — one stabilizes the mood architecture, the other regulates the emotional tone within it.

💪 Why exercise changes everything

Exercise increases BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) — literally a growth fertilizer for brain cells — and boosts serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine. Three sessions a week is clinically shown to be as effective as antidepressants for mood stabilization. It also reduces amygdala reactivity over time, meaning fewer and less intense rage/anxiety episodes the longer you keep it up.

🌿 Why you're not broken

Your brain learned to be this reactive for a reason. Hypervigilance and intense emotions often develop as adaptations — they protected you at some point. You're not starting over. Every time you use a tool instead of a reaction, you build a new neural pathway. That is real, measurable, biological change. You are just starting — and you're already doing it.
